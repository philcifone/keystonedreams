Fallingwater

The drive to our first stop Fallingwater by Frank Lloyd Wright in Mill Run, Stewart Township, Fayette County, Pennsylvania is a surprisingly quick ~270 mile drive across most of the state, almost entirely across its portion of US Interstate 76 West. It wasn't long before religious texts and scriptures painted on unused billboards and the sides of deteriorating barns line the highway:

"Everyone shall give account of himself to god."

"Who rules your life? Jesus Christ or self?"

The further we get into Central Pennsylvania the more pronounced the Appalachian Mountain range becomes. I've become so enamored by these Ancient ridgelines. Eons ago this mountain range would have rivaled the current Swiss Alps or Rocky Mountains, but time has eroded them to the glorified hills they are today. They still feel gargantuan, eminence from the shadow of it's former self. Yet, I know my two legs and feet could climb them with the pair of Keens I have in the car, and a backpack full of water and stomach full of trail mix.

I speed my little green Subaru hatchback alongside them, rubber tires slowly disintegrating. They leave cancerous microscopic bits and pieces of itself as they wear along the asphalt road. The bits and pieces wait for rain water to wash them away to contaminate the soil of the trees on the mountain I'm admiring, and other parts unknown where they will reside for further eons. I try not to think about this but I had just read a book about trees, and it affected me deeply.

Fallingwater was one of the most intriguing architectural wonders I have ever experienced in person. You take an extended walk from the parking lot through a beautiful garden before rounding a corner where the house sort of jumps out at you through the rhododendron blanketed valley. The base of the house is integrated with the landscape and stone, the terraces of the house sort of mimic Bear Run, the creek/river it's built on top of. It has rained the last 4 days in row, and you can tell by the threatening force of the water rushing underneath the house.

The house is quintessential Frank Lloyd Wright. Compression and release as you walk through the door, everything is built in, and the furniture is entirely designed by him. Much of it feels much more modern than the 1937 birthday it has. Nearly all modern architecture I've ever experienced has been influenced by him, so it makes sense his designs specifically stand the test of time.

Having recently purchased a home for the first time, I have been intrigued by interior design and attempting to make the most out of our small, utilitarian space in more meaningful ways. Experiencing Fallingwater, where every square inch of the space is rendered with intent is nothing short of inspiring in that regard. Postcard images do not begin to do it justice.

The house is plagued with maintenance issues, as it is currently undergoing seven million dollars worth of renovations. The tour guide wasn't shy about talking about it and it's evident from the scaffolding in front of the guest house and absorbent towels over furniture in certain rooms. The house is 89 years old and one of the most iconic historic homes in America, and is a modern marvel of it's time. Maintenance costing a pretty penny is unavoidable.

What I think I enjoy most about the design and architecture, is how he keeps Nature at the forefront. In the kitchen, with the large window and stone wall. In the dining room, the stone wall and floor dominate the space. Imagine waking up on a Saturday morning, walking barefoot through the house on the cool stone floor to the kitchen for a warm cup of coffee and sipping on it as you stare out the window at Bear Run as it rushes through a the rhododendron valley. What a life that must have been for the original owners.

I'm astounded to learn that the house has welcomed guests such as Frida Kahlo and Albert Einstein. Its art collection inside was also notable. It includes numerous Picasso prints, Japanese Woodblock prints, a Diego Rivera painting, a stunning 15th century wood carving in Mrs. Kaufman's bedroom, amazing Frank Lloyd Wright designed furniture, and much more.

It was 'prohibited' to take pictures upstairs, purely due to the space constraint. I imagine social media and the incessant need to take video all the time has ruined many of these sorts of places for us respectful photographers, but I understand. So naturally I hung back to take some using my phone inconspicuously. This bathroom was the only picture of note, and I love the bottles of after shave, super classy.

Plenty has been said about this architectural marvel and its history that I don't wish to regurgitate. Be sure to experience this magnificent house in person. I love that one of the countries most treasured works of architectural design is nestled in the woods of the Keystone State.

We arrive in Pittsburgh after sitting in standstill traffic on the bridge and arrived with just enough sunlight to take pictures of the skyline on the observation decks along Grandview Avenue. The weather couldn't make up it's mind today and we are welcomed to this cloudy but stunning view of a city in a valley and it's rivers and bridges with heavy but transient sleet and freezing rain as the light escapes and night falls on the first day full of bucket list dream museum/historic home visit.
